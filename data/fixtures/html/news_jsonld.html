City council approves riverside flood barrier after two-year review

By Maria Okafor and Daniel Reyes

The city council voted on Thursday evening to approve a flood barrier along the eastern bank of the river, ending a review that lasted a little more than two years and drew hundreds of written comments from residents.

The barrier will run for almost three kilometres between the old grain terminal and the railway bridge. Engineers told the council that the structure is designed to hold back a surge of the size recorded during the winter storm of 2013, when water entered more than four hundred homes in the lower districts.

Nine councillors voted in favour of the plan and four voted against it. Those who opposed the project said they did not dispute the need for protection, but argued that the chosen design would cut off public access to a stretch of the waterfront that is popular with walkers and anglers.

"We listened to those concerns and changed the design twice," said the chair of the planning committee, Helen Marsh. "The final version keeps the footpath open and adds two new crossing points. It is not perfect, but it is a great deal better than leaving people unprotected for another decade."

The project is expected to cost about forty-two million, according to figures published by the finance department. Roughly half of that amount will come from a national resilience fund, and the remainder will be borrowed by the city and repaid over twenty-five years.

Construction is scheduled to begin next spring and to take three years. The council said it would publish a progress report every six months and hold a public meeting in the affected districts before each phase of the work begins.

A residents' association from the lower districts welcomed the decision. Its secretary, Tom Bailey, said that many families had been unable to obtain affordable insurance since the last flood and that the vote gave them a reason to stay in the area.
