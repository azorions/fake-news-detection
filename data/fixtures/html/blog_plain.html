Why our village bakery switched to a wood-fired oven

When we took over the bakery three winters ago, the first thing every customer asked was whether we would keep the old electric deck oven. For two years we did, mostly because replacing it felt like a luxury we could not justify.

That changed last spring, when the heating elements failed for the third time in a year and the repair quote came close to the price of a small car. We sat down with a notebook and worked out what a wood-fired oven would cost to build, to fuel and to learn.

The numbers surprised us. Timber offcuts from the sawmill down the road are cheap and close, and a masonry oven holds its heat so well that one firing in the early morning carries us through three full bakes. The harder part was the learning. Our first loaves were scorched on the bottom and pale on top, and it took a month of patient adjustments before the crust came out the way we wanted.

The bread is different now. The crumb is more open, the crust is thicker, and the loaves keep for a day longer. Not everyone prefers it, and we still bake soft rolls in a small electric oven for the customers who do. But most mornings the queue reaches the door before we open, and that never happened before.

If you are thinking about making the same change, visit a bakery that already works this way and spend a full shift there. The romance fades quickly at four in the morning, and it is better to find that out before you start laying bricks.
